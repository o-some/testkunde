01 / FOUNDATIONS

A good start feels controlled.

First, get familiar with your stance, body tension and basic movement patterns. Higher supports, shorter movements or assistance make the starting point adaptable.

  • Simple warm-up and gentle practice movements
  • Squats, incline push-ups and core control
  • Feedback before increasing difficulty

You don't need to be able to do a pull-up to start.

02 / STRENGTH & CONTROL

A controlled repetition counts.

Once the foundations are in place, you can adjust leverage, range of motion or repetitions. Choose a variation that lets you stay in control.

  • Combine pulling, pushing, legs and core
  • Suitable variations rather than random maximum efforts
  • Rest and adjustments are part of training

Progress can also mean more control in the same exercise.

03 / SKILL PREPARATION

Big skills grow from small building blocks.

Handstands and more complex support positions build on solid foundations. Body tension, controlled positions and targeted preparation come first.

  • Assess foundations and prerequisites together
  • Short technique blocks with a clear task
  • Demanding variations only with enough control

No fixed timeline or promise of specific skills.

UNDERSTANDING CALISTHENICS

CLEAR TERMS.
MORE DIRECTION.

You'll hear a few words again and again in training. Here's what they mean at a glance.

Repetition
One complete execution of an exercise, for example lowering and standing up once.
Set
Several repetitions or a hold before you take a break.
Tempo
The speed of individual movement phases. A slow lowering phase is an example of deliberately controlled speed.
Range of motion
The distance you move through in an exercise. It should suit your current control.
Regression
An easier variation, such as a push-up on a higher stable support.
Progression
An adjusted increase in challenge, such as different leverage or a greater controlled range of motion.

06 / GOOD TO KNOW

YOUR QUESTIONS.
CLEAR ANSWERS.

What is calisthenics?

Calisthenics mainly uses your own bodyweight. Bars, parallel bars or rings can support exercises. The focus is controlled movement, strength and body tension.

Do I have to attend all three sessions?

The three sessions form the weekly schedule. Training frequency and intensity should fit your level and recovery.

Does everyone train at the same difficulty?

Everyone can work on the same movement pattern using different variations. Control, experience and current capacity guide the choice.

How do I plan my first session?

Choose a session and your level in the starting-point planner. This helps you identify when you'd like to begin and your current foundations.

How do I prepare for outdoor training?

Wear suitable sportswear and shoes, bring water and check the weather before training. Adapt clothing and effort to the conditions.

Can I view the free exercises without signing up?

Yes. All six illustrated guides are directly accessible. Click “Open illustrated guide”. The full instructions appear on this website, and you can also view the illustration at full size.

Do I need to be fit already?

No. Start with the foundations and increase the load gradually. Different variations let you start at your current level.

What does the €50 include?

The three guided weekly sessions are included. No more than ten people train per session.

What should I bring?

Sportswear, suitable shoes, water and a towel if needed. Personal training aids are optional.

What does the starting-point planner do?

Choose a session and your level. The planner shows your choice without requesting or sending personal data. It does not reserve a training place.

What about symptoms or injuries?

If you have pain, existing conditions or uncertainty, seek medical advice before participating. Stop exercises if symptoms occur. Training does not replace medical advice or treatment.

01 / LEGS

Squat

Tap or click the image to view full size in a new tab. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Find your stance

    Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart. Turn your toes slightly out if it feels natural. Reach your arms forward.

  2. Lower with control

    Bend at the hips and knees. Keep your heels down and knees aligned with your toes. Only go as low as you can stay stable and pain-free.

  3. Stand back up

    Push through your whole foot to stand up. Breathe out as you rise and keep your core stable.

Getting familiar

Start with 5–8 calm repetitions to get familiar. Stop the set before your technique deteriorates.

Make it easier

Bend less deeply or lower towards a stable chair. The chair must be secure and unable to slide.

Watch out for

Lifting heels, knees collapsing inward or going deeper than you can control.

02 / UPPER BODY

Incline push-up

Tap or click the image to view full size in a new tab. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Set up your support

    Place your hands about shoulder-width apart on a fixed, non-slip elevated surface. Step your feet back. Keep your head, torso and legs in a fairly straight line.

  2. Lower slowly

    Bend your arms with control and bring your chest towards the support. Point your elbows diagonally back instead of flaring them widely sideways.

  3. Push back up

    Push through your hands to rise. Keep your body line stable and breathe out as you push.

Getting familiar

Try 4–6 controlled repetitions first. Choose a height that lets you lower and push back up safely.

Make it easier

Use a higher stable support or a solid wall. A more upright body position usually makes the variation easier.

Watch out for

Avoid a wobbling bench, sagging hips or moving only your head towards the support. Don't use wheelchairs, movable furniture or slippery surfaces.

03 / CORE

Bird Dog

Tap or click the image to view full size in a new tab. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Find your all-fours position

    Place your hands under your shoulders and knees under your hips. Look down and keep your back neutral.

  2. Extend opposite limbs

    Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back. Keep your pelvis as level as possible and avoid arching your lower back. Keep breathing calmly.

  3. Return & switch

    Slowly bring your hand and knee back. Switch sides. Control matters more than height or speed.

Getting familiar

Start with 3–5 slow repetitions per side. A brief stable position is enough; you don't need to hold as long as possible.

Make it easier

Move only one arm or one leg first. Reduce the range until you can keep your core still.

Watch out for

Avoid rotating your pelvis, raising your leg too high, overextending your back or holding your breath.

04 / LEGS

Glute Bridge

Click the image for full size. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Starting position

    Lie on your back. Place your feet about hip-width apart, bend your knees and rest your arms by your sides.

  2. Raise your pelvis

    Press your feet into the ground and lift your pelvis with control. Shoulders, hips and knees form an approximate line. Avoid excessive lower-back arching.

  3. Return calmly

    Lower your pelvis slowly. Breathe evenly and choose a range that feels stable and pain-free.

Getting familiar

Try 5–8 calm repetitions while you can control the movement.

Make it easier

Lift your pelvis only a little. A mat can make the surface more comfortable.

Watch out for

Don't thrust upward with momentum, overextend your back or hold your breath while lifting.

05 / LEGS

Calf raise

Click the image for full size. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Stand securely

    Stand about hip-width apart on a flat, non-slip floor. Lightly use a fixed support with your hands if you need balance assistance.

  2. Raise your heels

    Lift both heels with control onto the balls of your feet. Keep your torso upright and knees gently extended.

  3. Lower slowly

    Lower your heels calmly to the floor. Stay within a safe range and keep breathing.

Getting familiar

Try 5–8 slow repetitions on flat ground.

Make it easier

Use a fixed support and lift your heels less.

Watch out for

Don't bounce or stand on unstable objects. You don't need to start on the edge of a step.

06 / CORE

Kneeling forearm plank

Click the image for full size. AI-generated illustration.

  1. Find your support

    Rest your forearms on a mat with your elbows under your shoulders. Keep your knees on the ground.

  2. Build your line

    Position your body so shoulders, hips and knees form a fairly straight line. Look down, keep your neck neutral and avoid sagging.

  3. Hold briefly & release

    Keep breathing calmly. Hold only while you can control the position. Release and sit back comfortably.

Getting familiar

Begin with a brief stable position, for example 5–10 seconds. Stop sooner if your posture deteriorates.

Make it easier

Shorten the hold or first try a forearm plank on a fixed elevated support.

Watch out for

Don't let your hips sag, lift your head or hold your breath.
