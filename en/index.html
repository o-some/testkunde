Understanding calisthenics and flexibility

STAY MOBILE.
RECOVER WITH INTENT.

Strength needs freedom of movement. Progress needs recovery. Connect controlled mobility, appropriate training loads and rest in your routine.

01

Build flexibility

Regular stretching can increase your range of motion. Active mobility also trains control within that range. Greater depth is useful only when you can use it without compensation or pain.

02

Reduce injury risks

Stretching alone does not reliably prevent injuries. Appropriate exercise variations, gradual loading and controlled technique matter. Include a preparatory warm-up; it cannot guarantee injury-free training.

03

Take recovery seriously

Gentle stretching can feel pleasant after training. Evidence does not convincingly support faster strength recovery or reduced soreness. Make room for sleep, food, fluids and recovery between demanding sessions.

Supercompensation · explained simply

THE STIMULUS STARTS IT.
REST IS PART OF IT.

A demanding session initially leaves you fatigued. With recovery, your body can respond to repeated, appropriate training stimuli and improve its capacity. The simplified supercompensation model describes this relationship.

  1. 01

    Training stimulus

    A challenge matched to your current ability.

  2. 02

    Fatigue

    Performance may temporarily drop. Going harder is not automatically better now.

  3. 03

    Recovery

    Manage load, sleep and replenish energy.

  4. 04

    Adaptation

    Across sessions you may become more capable and gradually adjust the next challenge.

A thinking tool, not a timer: muscles, tendons and the nervous system adapt differently. There is no universal 48-hour window or guaranteed performance jump after every workout.

MAKE IT PART OF YOUR WEEK.

A simple starting point – Taylan adapts the volume and selection to your mobility and readiness.

Before training: mobilise

Start with easy walking or light movement. Mobilise wrists and shoulders with small, controlled circles and pain-free movements. Rehearse the first strength exercise using a much easier variation before increasing load. Long, intense stretches do not replace this warm-up.

Separately or afterwards: stretch gently

For the chest: place a forearm against a stable wall and slowly turn away, keeping the shoulder relaxed. For the calf: place hands against a wall, step one foot back and keep that heel down. Pause briefly at a mild stretch, breathe calmly and release slowly. Do not bounce or push into joint pain. Work both sides within a comfortable range.

Between sessions: check recovery

Allow easier days or rest between highly demanding sessions for the same muscle groups. Three weekly sessions need not all be maximal. During your warm-up, check your movement control and whether an easy variation feels normal. Reduce volume if performance clearly drops; stop for sharp pain and seek professional assessment.

Scientific context

Research supports improved flexibility with regular stretching. Stretching alone has not demonstrated reliable injury prevention or faster recovery. Many studied sports differ from calisthenics. This routine is practical guidance, not individual treatment.

06 / GOOD TO KNOW

YOUR QUESTIONS.
CLEAR ANSWERS.

What is calisthenics?

Calisthenics mainly uses your own bodyweight. Bars, parallel bars or rings can support exercises. The focus is controlled movement, strength and body tension.

Do I have to attend all three sessions?

The three sessions form the weekly schedule. Training frequency and intensity should fit your level and recovery.

Does everyone train at the same difficulty?

Everyone can work on the same movement pattern using different variations. Control, experience and current capacity guide the choice.

How do I plan my first session?

Choose a session and your level in the starting-point planner. This helps you identify when you'd like to begin and your current foundations.

How do I prepare for outdoor training?

Wear suitable sportswear and shoes, bring water and check the weather before training. Adapt clothing and effort to the conditions.

Can I view the free exercises without signing up?

Yes. All three illustrated guides are directly accessible. Click “View technique”. The full instructions appear on this website, and you can also view the illustration at full size.

Do I need to be fit already?

No. Start with the foundations and increase the load gradually. Different variations let you start at your current level.

What does the €50 include?

The three guided weekly sessions are included. No more than ten people train per session.

What should I bring?

Sportswear, suitable shoes, water and a towel if needed. Personal training aids are optional.

What does the starting-point planner do?

Choose a session and your level. The planner shows your choice without requesting or sending personal data. It does not reserve a training place.

What about symptoms or injuries?

If you have pain, existing conditions or uncertainty, seek medical advice before participating. Stop exercises if symptoms occur. Training does not replace medical advice or treatment.

01 / PUSH

Push-ups

AI-created image sequence. Follow the movement cues and your individually controlled, pain-free range.

  1. Set your position

    Place hands about shoulder-width apart with comfortable finger direction. Align head, torso and legs; lightly brace your abdomen and glutes.

  2. Lower with control

    Bring your chest towards the floor. Direct elbows diagonally back rather than far out to the sides. Use only a range you can hold without pain.

  3. Press as one unit

    Push the floor away while keeping your body line. Allow natural shoulder-blade movement. Extend arms with control, without snapping the elbows.

An easier start

Place hands on a stable raised surface or a wall. The support must not tip or slide.

What to watch

Avoid sagging through the lower back or lowering only the head. Have the option adjusted professionally for wrist symptoms.

Manage the load

Begin with a few controlled repetitions. Finish when alignment or control deteriorates.

Scientific basis

A force-platform study (23 participants) found lower ground reaction forces with elevated hands than in standard push-ups. A shoulder study (16 participants) examined less favourable scapular positions with elevated upper arms. Neither proves injury-free exercise.

  • Ebben et al., 2011 · Push-up variations
  • Suprak et al., 2013 · Scapular kinematics
  • ACE · Push-up technique

02 / PULL

Pull-ups

AI-created image sequence. Follow the movement cues and your individually controlled, pain-free range.

  1. Grip & starting position

    Use a secure bar and a moderate, roughly shoulder-width overhand grip. Find a quiet hang; extend arms only as far as you can control without symptoms.

  2. Pull smoothly

    Keep your trunk stable and guide elbows down. Allow your shoulder blades to move. Clear the bar with your chin only if you can do so without craning your neck.

  3. Return slowly

    Lower without dropping. Avoid swinging and kipping. Adjust the range or assistance when control is insufficient.

An easier start

Use foot assistance at a suitable low bar. Agree on setup and support with the coach; do not climb onto unstable objects.

What to watch

Avoid forcing an extremely wide grip, pulling behind the neck or jerking from the bottom.

Manage the load

Use enough assistance for a few smooth repetitions. Reducing assistance comes later.

Scientific basis

Kinematic measurements and a shoulder model show that grip options change shoulder mechanics and muscle loading. These small laboratory studies offer guidance; they do not prove that any grip prevents injury in everyone.

  • Prinold & Bull, 2016 · Pull-up kinematics
  • Urbanczyk et al., 2020 · Shoulder loading
  • NASM · Pull-up technique

03 / SUPPORT

Parallel-bar dips

AI-created image sequence. Follow the movement cues and your individually controlled, pain-free range.

  1. Find a stable support

    Grip fixed, appropriately spaced bars. Extend arms with control and brace your trunk. Avoid passively sinking through the shoulders; use agreed assistance if needed.

  2. Control your depth

    Bend elbows back and lower smoothly. A slight forward lean is possible. Stop before uncomfortable shoulder stretch; maximum depth is not required.

  3. Press up smoothly

    Push through your hands to regain stable support. Do not bounce out of the bottom. Extend arms with control and keep breathing.

An easier start

Use foot assistance on suitable low bars and practise a short range first. Have the coach check the setup.

What to watch

Bench dips are not automatically a gentle alternative. Depth, fatigue and extra weight increase demands; seek advice first with shoulder symptoms.

Manage the load

Learn stable support, then try a few assisted repetitions. Finish before your shoulders lose support.

Scientific basis

A study of 13 experienced men found greater shoulder extension in bench dips than bar and ring dips. It measured movement and muscle activity, not long-term injury rates. Assistance and limited depth here are practical adaptations, not a safety guarantee.

  • McKenzie et al., 2022 · Bench, bar and ring dips
